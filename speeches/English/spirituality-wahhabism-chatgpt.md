# SPIRITUALITY IS FAR FROM WAHHABISM

**بِسْمِ اللهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيْمِ**

All praise belongs to Allah, the Lord of all worlds. May peace and blessings be upon our beloved Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, his family, his companions, and all those who follow his noble path.

Respected judges, honourable teachers, distinguished guests, and my dear brothers and sisters,

**Can a heart be full of religious words, yet empty of love? Can a person speak constantly about religion, yet fail to show mercy? Can the body perform acts of worship while the heart remains imprisoned by pride?**

These questions take us to the very heart of my topic:

**“Spirituality Is Far from Wahhabism.”**

Let me begin with a clarification. I am not here to judge the faith or sincerity of individual Muslims. Nor should disagreement become an excuse for insulting or humiliating anyone. My concern is with a theological tendency associated with Wahhabism, particularly where an emphasis on outward conformity, restrictive interpretations and rejection of established spiritual traditions leaves insufficient room for the rich tradition of *Ihsan* preserved by Ahl al-Sunnah wa’l-Jama‘ah.

Dear audience,

Islam is not merely a religion of outward forms. It is a religion of the body, the mind and, above all, the heart.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ explained the highest spiritual station in the famous Hadith of Jibril:

**أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّكَ تَرَاهُ، فَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاكَ**

“To worship Allah as though you see Him; and if you do not see Him, indeed He sees you.”

This is *Ihsan*—the excellence of faith, the consciousness of Allah, the awakening of the soul.

Notice something beautiful: the Prophet ﷺ did not describe spirituality as a beard, a garment, a slogan or an argument. He described it as a living awareness of Allah.

Allah says in the Holy Qur’an:

**قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَنْ زَكَّاهَا**

“Successful indeed is the one who purifies the soul.”

(Surah Ash-Shams, 91:9)

This is the foundation of the spiritual journey: *Tazkiyat al-Nafs*—purification of the self.

My dear brothers and sisters,

The great Sunni tradition of Tasawwuf has never been a licence to abandon the Shariah. Its authentic purpose is to live the Shariah with sincerity, discipline and love. It seeks to remove arrogance from the heart, hypocrisy from intention, hatred from relationships and heedlessness from worship.

Imam al-Ghazali, in his monumental work *Ihya’ ‘Ulum al-Din*, devoted profound attention to the diseases of the heart and the qualities that save it. His message reminds us that religious knowledge must transform the human being, not merely decorate the tongue.

But what happens when religion becomes only a debate about appearances? What happens when every difference becomes an accusation, every inherited practice becomes a suspicion, and every expression of love is treated with hostility?

We must be careful here: not every person associated with Wahhabism rejects spirituality, and Muslims may disagree over particular practices for sincere reasons. But wherever religious discourse neglects the purification of the heart, dismisses the legitimate spiritual heritage of Sunni scholarship, or turns disagreement into contempt, it moves away from the beauty of *Ihsan*.

The Prophet ﷺ gave us a remarkable measure of human worth:

**إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَنْظُرُ إِلَى صُوَرِكُمْ وَأَمْوَالِكُمْ، وَلَكِنْ يَنْظُرُ إِلَى قُلُوبِكُمْ وَأَعْمَالِكُمْ**

“Indeed, Allah does not look at your appearance or your wealth, but He looks at your hearts and your deeds.”

(Sahih Muslim, 2564)

What a profound lesson! The real question is not simply how religious we appear, but how sincere we are; not merely how much we know, but how beautifully that knowledge shapes our character.

Look at the spiritual giants of our tradition: Imam Junayd al-Baghdadi, Imam al-Ghazali, Rabia al-Adawiyya and the countless scholars and saints whose lives were illuminated by knowledge, worship, humility and love.

They remind us that the path to Allah is not a journey of self-importance. It is a journey of self-purification.

And let me express this thought in a few lines:

*Let knowledge make the heart more kind,*  
*Let worship leave no pride behind.*  
*Let love be seen in what we do,*  
*And mercy shine in all we pursue.*

Dear audience,

We do not need a religion that makes us look superior to other people. We need a faith that makes us more humble before Allah and more compassionate towards His creation.

If our worship does not soften our hearts, let us examine our worship. If our knowledge makes us arrogant, let us examine our knowledge. If our religious commitment makes us cruel, let us question whether we have understood its purpose.

**The mosque should be a place where hearts are united in the remembrance of Allah, not a battlefield where Muslims are humiliated.**

**The tongue that remembers Allah should not become a weapon against His servants.**

And the heart that longs for the Prophet ﷺ should learn to reflect his mercy.

Let us therefore preserve the spiritual inheritance of Ahl al-Sunnah wa’l-Jama‘ah: a tradition in which Shariah and spirituality walk together, knowledge is crowned with humility, worship is illuminated by sincerity, and love is reflected in character.

I would like to conclude with one thought:

**A religion without knowledge loses its direction. Knowledge without humility loses its beauty. And worship without a purified heart loses its deepest sweetness.**

May Allah grant us knowledge that benefits, hearts that are pure, worship that is sincere, love for His beloved Messenger ﷺ, and character that reflects the mercy of Islam.

May He unite our hearts upon truth, protect us from arrogance and hatred, and make us people of both sound knowledge and beautiful spirituality.

وَصَلَّى اللهُ تَعَالَى عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَى آلِهِ وَصَحْبِهِ وَسَلَّمَ.

Thank you. **Assalamu Alaikum wa Rahmatullahi wa Barakatuh.**

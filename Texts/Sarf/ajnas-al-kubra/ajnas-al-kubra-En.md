## Scholastic Proem & Semantic Transitivity (*al-Lāzim wal-Mutaʿaddī*)

<div dir="rtl" lang="ar">
الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ، وَالْعَاقِبَةُ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ، وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى سَيِّدِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ، وَآلِهِ وَصَحْبِهِ أَجْمَعِينَ. وَبَعْدُ: فَاعْلَمْ يَا أَخِي -أَسْعَدَكَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى وَإِيَّانَا فِي الدَّارَيْنِ- أَنَّ الْأَفْعَالَ مِنْ حَيْثُ الْمَعْنَى نَوْعَانِ: لَازِمٌ، وَمُتَعَدٍّ.
</div>

**Translation & Commentary:**  
*All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all the worlds; the ultimate triumph is for the God-conscious; and blessings and peace be upon our Master Muḥammad, his family, and all his companions.*  
*To proceed: Know, O brother—may Allah Almighty grant happiness to you and to us in both abodes—that verbs, with respect to semantic valency and syntactic reach, are of two categories:*
1. **Intransitive (*al-Lāzim*):** That which does not surpass the agent (*Fāʿil*) to affect a direct object (*Mafʿūl bihi*). Consequently, neither a true passive voice (*Mabnī lil-Majhūl*) nor a passive participle (*Ism al-Mafʿūl*) can be constructed from it except via prepositional attachment (*Ṣilah*). Examples: <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">حَسُنَ زَيْدٌ</span> (*Zayd became handsome*), <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">قَعَدَ عَمْرٌو</span> (*ʿAmr sat*).
2. **Transitive (*al-Mutaʿaddī*):** That which surpasses the agent directly to govern an objective complement without the mediation of a preposition. From it are derived active forms, passive forms, active participles, and passive participles. Example: <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ عَمْرًا</span> (*Zayd struck ʿAmr*).

Furthermore, with respect to radical root structure, verbs are either **trilateral (*thulāthī*)** or **quadrilateral (*rubāʿī*)**, each being either **unaugmented (*mujarrad*)** or **augmented (*mazīd fīh*)**.

## Chapter I: The Six Canonical Doors of Unaugmented Trilateral Verbs

By evaluating all possible vowel permutations of the medial radical consonant (*ʿAyn al-Fiʿl*) in both the perfective and imperfective forms, classical Arabic grammarians systematically established **six canonical gates**:

1. **Door I: *Fatḥu Ḍamm* (فَعَلَ - يَفْعُلُ):**  
   - Exemplar: <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">نَصَرَ يَنْصُرُ</span> (*naṣara - yanṣuru* - to help). Typically transitive; occasionally intransitive (*qaʿada*).
2. **Door II: *Fatḥu Kasr* (فَعَلَ - يَفْعِلُ):**  
   - Exemplar: <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ</span> (*ḍaraba - yaḍribu* - to strike). Typically transitive; occasionally intransitive (*jalasa*).
3. **Door III: *Fatḥatān* (فَعَلَ - يَفْعَلُ):**  
   - Exemplar: <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">فَتَحَ يَفْتَحُ</span> (*fataḥa - yaftaḥu* - to open). Phonetically conditioned by a guttural consonant at the second or third radical.
4. **Door IV: *Kasru Fatḥ* (فَعِلَ - يَفْعَلُ):**  
   - Exemplar: <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">عَلِمَ يَعْلَمُ</span> (*ʿalima - yaʿlamu* - to know). Transitive (*ʿalima*) and intransitive (*fariḥa*).
5. **Door V: *Ḍammu Ḍamm* (فَعُلَ - يَفْعُلُ):**  
   - Exemplar: <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">كَرُمَ يَكْرُمُ</span> (*karuma - yakrumu* - to be noble). Strictly intransitive, denoting congenital dispositions and innate qualities.
6. **Door VI: *Kasratān* (فَعِلَ - يَفْعِلُ):**  
   - Exemplar: <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">حَسِبَ يَحْسِبُ</span> (*ḥasiba - yaḥsibu* - to reckon). The rarest of the six doors in linguistic frequency.

## Chapter II: Anomalous and Irregular Verbs (*al-Shādh*)

Certain verbs deviate from standard phonological analogy (*Qiyās*) yet are authentically attested through classical transmission (*Samāʿ*):
1. **Forms exhibiting *Faʿala - Yafʿalu* without throat consonants:**  
   Exemplar: <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">أَبَى يَأْبَى</span> (*abā - yaʾbā* - to refuse). The hamzah is its initial radical (*Fāʾ*), not its medial or final radical.
2. **Defective verbs maintaining double kasrah:**  
   Exemplars: *Wamiqa - yamiqu* (to cherish), *waritha - yarithu* (to inherit), *waliya - yalī* (to govern). The analogical rule for *faʿila* prescribes a fatḥah in the imperfective (*yaʿlamu*).

## Chapter III: The Complete 20-Term Derivational Paradigm

Taking **Door I (*Naṣara*)** as the exhaustive paradigm, the 20 morphological derivational states comprise:
1. *Māḍī Maʿlūm:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">نَصَرَ</span> (Active Perfective)
2. *Muḍāriʿ Maʿlūm:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">يَنْصُرُ</span> (Active Imperfective)
3. *Maṣdar Muṭlaq:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">نَصْرًا</span> (Absolute Verbal Noun)
4. *Ism al-Fāʿil:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">نَاصِرٌ</span> (Active Participle)
5. *Māḍī Majhūl:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">نُصِرَ</span> (Passive Perfective)
6. *Muḍāriʿ Majhūl:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">يُنْصَرُ</span> (Passive Imperfective)
7. *Ism al-Mafʿūl:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">مَنْصُورٌ</span> (Passive Participle)
8. *Jaḥd bi-Lam:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">لَمْ يَنْصُرْ</span> (Past Negation with Apocopate)
9. *Nafy bi-Lā:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">لَا يَنْصُرُ</span> (Imperfective Negation)
10. *Nafy bi-Lan:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">لَنْ يَنْصُرَ</span> (Categorical Subjunctive Negation)
11. *Amr bi-Lām:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">لِيَنْصُرْ</span> (Jussive Third-Person Command)
12. *Amr Ḥāḍir:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">انْصُرْ</span> (Second-Person Imperative)
13. *Nahy Ghāʾib:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">لَا يَنْصُرْ</span> (Third-Person Prohibitive)
14. *Nahy Ḥāḍir:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">لَا تَنْصُرْ</span> (Second-Person Prohibitive)
15. *Nūn al-Tawkīd al-Thaqīlah:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">لَيَنْصُرَنَّ / انْصُرَنَّ</span> (Heavy Corroborative Suffix)
16. *Nūn al-Tawkīd al-Khafīfah:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">لَيَنْصُرَنْ / انْصُرَنْ</span> (Light Corroborative Suffix)
17. *Ism al-Zamān wal-Makān:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">مَنْصَرٌ</span> (Noun of Time and Place)
18. *Ism al-Ālah:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">مِنْصَرٌ / مِنْصَارٌ</span> (Noun of Instrument)
19. *Ism al-Marrah:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">نَصْرَةً</span> (Noun of Single Instance / Nomen vicis)
20. *Ism al-Nawʿ:* <span dir="rtl" lang="ar">نِصْرَةً</span> (Noun of Manner / Nomen speciei)

## Chapter IV: Augmented Trilateral Verbs & Morpho-Semantic Values

Augmentation is guided by the foundational linguistic axiom: *“An increase in morphemic structure denotes an increase in semantic meaning”* (*Ziyādat al-mabnā tadullu ʿalā ziyādat al-maʿnā*):
1. **Bāb al-Ifʿāl (*Afʿala*):** Transitivization (*Taʿdiyah*), entering a time or region (*Aṣbaḥa, Aʿraqa*), deprivation (*Aʿjama*).
2. **Bāb al-Tafʿīl (*Faʿʿala*):** Intensive action (*Qaṭṭaʿa*), transitivization (*Farraḥa*), attributing a quality (*Fassaqa*).
3. **Bāb al-Mufāʿalah (*Fāʿala*):** Reciprocal interaction between entities (*Qātala, Ḍāraba*).
4. **Bāb al-Infiʿāl (*Infaʿala*):** Reflexive compliance (*Muṭāwaʿah* - e.g. *Inkasara*). Exclusively intransitive.
5. **Bāb al-Iftiʿāl (*Iftaʿala*):** Personal acquisition and effort (*Iktasaba*).
6. **Bāb al-Istifʿāl (*Istafʿala*):** Petition/request (*Istaghfara*), metamorphosis into an essence (*Istaḥjara*).
